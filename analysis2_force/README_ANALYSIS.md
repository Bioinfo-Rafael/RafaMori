# IRC / Reaction Force Analysis — implementation record

## 1. Analysis overview

This pipeline compares the MoriPF and RAFA16 Gaussian IRC trajectories using electronic energy, Reaction Force, Reaction Force Constant, configured distances/angles, reference-coordinate distributions, Cartesian atomic forces, and force–motion products. All outputs are regenerated by `python analysis2_force/run_analysis.py`; figures are not edited manually.

The optimized reactant LOGs supplied for this run contain 44 atoms, whereas the IRC and product LOGs contain 57 atoms. Therefore the requested optimized-reactant-zero activation/reaction energies are retained as arithmetic outputs but marked `valid_for_physical_interpretation=false`. The IRC-internal derivatives, geometry analysis, Cartesian forces, and force–motion analyses remain usable subject to the numerical caveats below.

## 2. IRC coordinate convention

The coordinate convention is applied immediately after parsing, before sorting or any derivative:

MoriPF retains the established analysis orientation that was already in use before this rebuild. In parser coordinates this is

$$R_{\mathrm{MoriPF}}=-R_{\mathrm{parser}},$$

$$R_{\mathrm{RAFA16}}=-R_{\mathrm{original}}.$$

`coordinate_multiplier` is explicitly `-1` for both parser streams in `config/analysis2_config.yaml`: for MoriPF this preserves the pre-existing analysis orientation, while for RAFA16 it implements the newly mandated $R_{new}=-R_{original}$. The transformed coordinate is then used for energy interpolation, $dE/dR$, $dF/dR$, $dq/dR$, extrema, Cartesian motion, force–motion products, statistics, every CSV, and every figure. The TS is the known point $R=0$; it is not inferred from a force zero-crossing. Endpoint N–C distances independently verify the displayed direction: both systems run from the long, nonbonded N···C side at negative $R$ to the short N–C side at positive $R$.

Endpoint Kabsch RMSDs are retained only as a diagnostic. They do not override this explicit project convention because the reactant/IRC stoichiometries differ and the two possible RMSD scores are close.

## 4. Figure design convention

- White Matplotlib background; no `axvspan` or phase shading.
- Thin light-red dashed vertical lines at the system-specific $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ positions.
- Thin black/dark-grey dashed vertical line at $\beta=0$.
- No alpha/beta/gamma or structural-phase captions and no boundary entries in legends.
- MoriPF is blue (`#4C78A8`) and RAFA16 is orange (`#F58518`) in comparison plots.
- Computed IRC samples use small markers and solid lines; grids are light.
- In Figures 05 and 06, the reference mean is a thin grey-black line and mean ± population SD is a light translucent band.
- Median and IQR are not calculated or plotted.
- In Figure 06, color identifies an interaction pair and solid/dashed style identifies the two atoms within that pair.

## 6. Reaction Force

For the oriented path energy $E(R)$,

$$F(R)=-\frac{dE}{dR},$$

and this analysis uses the requested sign convention

$$\kappa(R)=\frac{dF}{dR}=-\frac{d^2E}{dR^2}.$$

`scipy.interpolate.make_smoothing_spline` is fit only to computed IRC energy points. `smoothing_spline_lambda: null` requests SciPy generalized cross-validation (GCV). $F$ and $\kappa$ are analytic derivatives of that spline. Raw nonuniform-grid finite differences are retained for comparison.

$\alpha$ is the continuous minimum of smoothed $F$ on $R<0$; $\beta=0$; $\gamma$ is the continuous maximum on $R>0$. Bounded continuous optimization determines $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ rather than snapping them to sampling points. The closest actual IRC samples and continuous/nearest energies are both saved. $\kappa(\alpha)$ and $\kappa(\gamma)$ are validated against zero.

## 7. Geometry-change analysis

For a distance or angle $q(R)$, the signed velocity is $dq/dR$. Figure 05 displays its magnitude,

$$\left|\frac{dq}{dR}\right|,$$

not the distance or angle itself. Distance panels use Å/R; the angle panel uses degree/R. Absolute values are appropriate for change-magnitude context, but signed derivatives are recomputed from $q(R)$ for the force–motion analysis in Section 9.

Covalent bonds are inferred from configurable covalent-radius cutoffs. Interfragment contacts are generated only across configured fragment pairs and retained when they reach the configurable 5 Å cutoff. Distances and angles are never mixed into one statistical population.

## 8. Cartesian atomic force

Gaussian provides each Cartesian atomic force vector

$$\mathbf F_i=(F_{ix},F_{iy},F_{iz})$$

in Hartree/Bohr. Figure 06 displays

$$|\mathbf F_i|=\sqrt{F_{ix}^2+F_{iy}^2+F_{iz}^2}.$$

This is not the scalar Reaction Force. Structures are rigidly Kabsch-aligned to the reconstructed TS reference for force–motion calculations; the same rotation is applied to force vectors, and translation is removed from positions.

## 9. Atomic / fragment force–motion analysis

The Cartesian chain-rule diagnostic is

$$C_i(R)=\mathbf F_i(R)\cdot\frac{d\mathbf r_i(R)}{dR}.$$

Coordinates are converted from Å to Bohr before the dot product, giving Hartree/R. `atomic_reaction_force_contribution_*.csv` stores the aligned force, three motion components, speed, and $C_i$. Figure 08 compares $\sum_i C_i$ with the energy-derived Reaction Force.

For the current files, the agreement is a strong sanity check:

| System | Compared points | Pearson r | normalized RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MoriPF | 64 | 0.999997 | 0.00261 |
| RAFA16 | 70 | 0.999721 | 0.02290 |

Despite this agreement, $C_i$ is called a Cartesian force–motion diagnostic rather than a unique rigorous atomic decomposition. Gaussian's IRC coordinate is mass-weighted, the parsed structures use standard orientations, and the supplied TS Cartesian structure is reconstructed. One missing-force TS row is preserved as NA.

Fragment contributions are sums of $C_i$ over lists resolved from configuration:

- MoriPF substituent side: 23–33; amine side: 45–57; coumarin side: 1–22 and 34–44.
- RAFA16 substituent side: 23–31; amine side: 43–55; coumarin side: 1–22, 32–42, and 56–57.
- MoriPF reaction-center diagnostic: 19, 20, 21, 29, 33, 45, 46, 47.
- RAFA16 reaction-center diagnostic: 19, 20, 21, 29, 31, 43, 44, 45.

The first three fragments form a disjoint 57-atom partition. `reaction_center` intentionally overlaps them and is a separate diagnostic.

For each configured distance $q=|\mathbf r_b-\mathbf r_a|$, Figure 10 uses the signed derivative and the symmetric two-atom generalized-force projection

$$F_{\mathrm{proj},q}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf F_b-\mathbf F_a)\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_{a\to b},$$

$$A_q(R)=F_{\mathrm{proj},q}(R)\frac{dq(R)}{dR}.$$

No absolute derivative is used in $A_q$; its sign is retained.

## Numerical caveats

- Reactant/IRC stoichiometry mismatch invalidates direct optimized-reactant activation and reaction energies.
- The IRC LOGs omit a TS Cartesian geometry; the parser reconstructs its geometry as the midpoint of the first structures from both directions while retaining the checkpoint TS energy.
- Geometry and motion derivatives immediately adjacent to $R=0$ are therefore less reliable.
- MoriPF contains one extrapolated terminal IRC coordinate because the final parsed point lacks a coordinate record.
- Spline derivatives and extrema depend on numerical smoothing; raw derivatives and nearest sampled extrema remain in CSV.
